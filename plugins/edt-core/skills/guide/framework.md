# The IBM Enterprise Design Thinking framework

Source: [ibm.com/training/enterprise-design-thinking/framework](https://www.ibm.com/training/enterprise-design-thinking/framework). The framework has three parts: the Principles, the Loop and the Keys. It treats problems and solutions as an ongoing conversation.

## 1. The Principles

| Principle | Meaning | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A focus on user outcomes** | Grow the business by helping users reach their goals. | **Managers:** distinguish users from clients, manage toward outcomes, and track user-outcome metrics. Net Promoter Score is one option. **Team members:** build empathy with users and understand their role. |
| **Restless reinvention** | Stay essential by treating everything as a prototype. | Commit to ideas early and iterate on them, instead of waiting for them to be perfect. |
| **Diverse empowered teams** | Move faster by empowering diverse teams to act. | **Managers:** assign team leadership, form self-contained teams and give them space. **Team members:** be inclusive, use conflict productively and take the initiative. Seek diversity of identity, experience and expertise. |

## 2. The Loop

Understand the present and envision the future in a continuous cycle of observing, reflecting and making. The phases can happen in any order, but a team should do all three. Observing and reflecting without making is analysis paralysis.

| Phase | What it means | Activities | Guiding questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Observe** | Immerse yourself in the real world. | Get to know users · Understand context · Uncover needs · Listen for feedback | Who are our users? What are their needs? What's their context? What's their feedback? |
| **Reflect** | Come together and look within. | Get to know each other · Align on intent · Uncover new insights · Plan ahead | What's our reality? What are we learning? Are we aligned? What's our plan? |
| **Make** | Give concrete form to abstract ideas. | Explore possibilities · Communicate ideas · Prototype concepts · Drive outcomes | What's possible? What's the concept? What's the story? How do we deliver? |

## 3. The Keys

The Keys keep large teams aligned on user outcomes.

### Hills: align us as teams
- Hills are statements of intent written as meaningful user outcomes.
- Each Hill has three parts: **Who** (a specific user), **What** (the enabled outcome) and **Wow** (the measurable, market-differentiating result).
- A Hill never describes an implementation.
- A project should have no more than three Hills, plus a **Foundation** that covers technical groundwork and fixes.
- A complex Hill can be split into Sub-Hills.
- The classic example is JFK's 1961 goal of landing a person on the Moon and returning them safely before the decade was out.
- Toolkit: `/edt-plan:hills`

### Playbacks: align us across time
- Playbacks bring stakeholders into the Loop in a safe space to tell stories and exchange feedback.
- Running one has three steps: invite stakeholders, tell your story, then listen for feedback and misalignment.
- Playbacks happen at these milestones:
  - **Hills Playback:** aligns everyone on the intent.
  - **Playback Zero:** aligns everyone on the concept. Draft rounds, such as "Playback −2" and "Playback −1", come before it.
  - **Delivery Playbacks:** show progress during delivery.
  - **Client Playbacks:** share work with clients, under NDA.
- Toolkit: `/edt-align:playbacks`

### Sponsor Users: align us with their reality
- Sponsor users are real-world users who regularly contribute their domain expertise to the team.
- Choose sponsor users who represent the target user, are personally invested in the outcome, and are available to collaborate.
- Involve them throughout the Loop: observe through their eyes, reflect together and make collaboratively.
- Aim for at least one sponsor user per Hill.
- Toolkit: `/edt-core:sponsor-users`
